# Step 10: Survey a field (survey mode only)

Write `metds/refs/<slug>_survey.md` — a standalone map of a field, read in tiers and organized by taxonomy. It needs no notes, no bib, not even a plan, and it writes neither `reference.bib` nor `refs_index.md`: promoting a paper it surfaced into the verified bib is one `/star-refs-reviewer <arxiv-id>` later — or the whole read-next list in one `/star-refs-reviewer add …`. `<slug>` is the plan's slug when a plan is the source, else the topic kebab-cased to ≤5 words.

1. **Source and profile.** Step 0 applies unchanged — profile stated before searching, incremental baseline read, language fixed. Also look for 1–3 existing surveys of the topic: their category schemes start the taxonomy axis, adapted to what this run actually collects, never adopted wholesale.
2. **Search wide.** Step 1's machinery as written — same queries from the profile, same fan-out cap and formats, same budgets and caching per `references/source_policy.md` — but the pool is kept, not cut to 15: a survey cites breadth. Keep the count ledger as the pool moves: found → deduplicated → screened → tiered, recorded in the survey's Scope section.
3. **Screen and tier.** Three reading tiers, by what each may support. **deep** — 8–12 papers at `method-and-results` or better; only these may fill comparison-table rows or carry reproduced numbers. **abstract** — 15–25 papers at `abstract-and-intro`, enough to place a paper on a branch and characterize it in a clause; fetch a page only where the search record carries no abstract. **record** — the rest, cited from their records' facts (title, venue, year) for breadth and trends, never characterized, never in a table. Tiering weighs relevance and impact score together; unlike the full pass, the score decides selection, so a branch's top-scored works belong in deep or abstract, not record. The list shows each score, partial until that page is read. Then the mode's one judgment call, in a single question: the profile, the taxonomy axis (and the axis rejected), and the tiered list with the deep tier recommended. `involve=low` takes the recommendation unasked and logs it in the decisions record (conventions §7.8); the full pass's core-set question stays mandatory.
4. **Read.** The deep and abstract tiers may fan out as Step 3 does — one paper per collector, per-host budget split as numbers per `references/source_policy.md`, one `raw/` prefix per paper — each returning the survey collector format in `references/refs_rubric.md`. Below 6 papers to read, skip the fan-out and read them in the main session. A paper whose fetched page names an official repository gets the same main-agent GitHub call as Step 3, completing its score — deep tier as a rule, an abstract-tier paper only when its page was fetched anyway; no page is fetched just to find a repo. No figure is captured here: figures are Step 3's, belonging to a note this mode does not write.
5. **Write from the pool, nothing else.** Draft each section only from what this run fetched and cached (`assets/survey_template.md`; Chinese: `assets/survey_template_zh.md`; grading: the survey section of `references/refs_rubric.md`). Every paper named in prose appears in the annotated references with its record URL and fetch date; every non-obvious claim carries an inline `[@key]` — same `Year_Method_FirstAuthor` shape as bib citekeys, resolved inside this file only, stable if the paper is later promoted into the bib; a claim with no cached source is cut or marked as this survey's own inference; a number never travels without its dataset and metric. Comparison-table rows are deep-tier papers only; a cell no cached source fills is `—`.
6. **Self-check.** Re-open 5 random claim–citation pairs against the cache; a claim its source does not support at that scope is rewritten or cut — the fix goes to the prose, never to the citation. Confirm every annotated-references link points at a page or record fetched this run.
7. **Frontmatter and overwrite protection.** Frontmatter: `type: survey`, `topic`, `language` (Step 0.4's rule), `generated` (real date), `sources` (the method source and its `updated`, when one was read), `papers` (per-tier counts), and `model_id` / `model_trail` (conventions §8). Re-running onto an existing `<slug>_survey.md` follows Step 9.4's rule verbatim.
8. **Digest.** ≤500 words: the source and profile, the count ledger, the branches written, the self-check result, and the routing — each read-next paper is one `/star-refs-reviewer <arxiv-id>`, the whole list one `/star-refs-reviewer add …`; positioning goes to `/star-plan-coach` §2 (Related Work & Positioning), or §1 (Problem Definition & Motivation) where the map shows the gap the plan wanted already closed, and `/star-idea-storm` where there is no plan yet to reopen; the full pass builds the notes and verified bib when the project needs them.

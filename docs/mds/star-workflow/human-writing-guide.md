# Clear writing in the research record

**Language:** English | [简体中文](human-writing-guide.zh-CN.md)

This guide applies shared checks for formulaic writing to STAR replies and narrative records.
The goal is clear, natural research prose, not authorship detection or detector evasion.
The [research workflow conventions](research-workflow-conventions.md) remain authoritative for evidence, state, ownership, and provenance.

## 1. Preserve content and provenance

A style edit may reorder, merge, split, or shorten prose, but it must leave the research record unchanged.

- Preserve facts, numbers, dates, thresholds, citations, quotations, attribution, source quality, statuses, decisions, uncertainty, negative results, open items, and technical distinctions.
- Preserve literal fields, identifiers, names, paths, commands, flags, environment keys, code, links, and done-criteria.
- Keep recorded facts, supported inferences, and open questions distinct.
  Also distinguish a source's claim from this project's judgment, a log report from an analysis finding, and sequence from causation.
- If information is missing or records conflict, keep the gap visible and route it to the owning workflow.
  Never supply a plausible detail, source, opinion, or conclusion.

Use one test: after the rewrite, can the next reader recover the same claim, status, uncertainty, decision, and source?

## 2. Match the writer and the record

Follow an author-confirmed sample when one exists.
Match its observable vocabulary, sentence movement, punctuation, transitions, qualification, and deliberate repetition without borrowing sentences or adding facts, opinions, humor, or disorder.
Without a sample, use restrained, direct technical prose.

- Lead with the answer, finding, change, or blocker, and end on the last useful fact, decision, command, or open item.
- Prefer canonical project terms and simple verbs.
  Name who measured, inferred, decided, or implemented something when agency affects interpretation.
- Use concrete evidence instead of claims of importance.
  State what changed, under which run or condition, and why it changes a decision.
- Keep the artifact's function visible: ideas and plans retain constraints and stop criteria; literature separates source claims from local synthesis; experiment prose retains provisional and adverse results; method and release documents compile verified sources instead of repairing gaps or selling hoped-for impact.
- Add personality only when the artifact and an author-confirmed source call for it.
  Technical and reference prose stays neutral.

## 3. Review pattern clusters

Treat these as editing signals, not banned forms or evidence of AI authorship.
Rewrite at paragraph scale when several signals accumulate, one template recurs, or a pattern introduces an unsupported claim.

| Review for | Rewrite toward |
| --- | --- |
| Inflated significance, sales language, name-dropping, unsupported superlatives, or stock optimism | The exact result and only its supported consequence. |
| Vague attribution, knowledge-limit disclaimers, or plausible guesses | A named, verified source and checkable proposition; otherwise an explicit gap or deletion. |
| Shallow analytical tails, abstract action chains, hidden actors, or stacked qualifiers | A direct fact–inference link, a clear actor where needed, and only evidentially necessary qualification. |
| Repeated “not X but Y,” forced triads or ranges, fake objections or alternatives, staged candor, slogans, or a claimed “deeper truth” | The real relation, constraint, or choice without drafting scaffolds. |
| Stock signposting, repeated headings, filler, greetings, praise, apologies, previews, service offers, or generic endings | The content itself and only navigation the reader needs. |
| Synonym cycling, stock diction, repeated openings, uniform cadence, dramatic fragments, excessive dashes, decorative emphasis, label-heavy lists, or emojis | Stable names and syntax, rhythm, or formatting that has a clear function. |

Do not ban a word, transition, passive construction, first person, long sentence, list, or dash in isolation.
Keep a form when it carries a real relation, preserves technical meaning, matches the author, or exposes required structure.
Never rewrite quotations, titles, code, data, or literal fields merely because they match a watched pattern.
Do not assign a numerical “human score.”

## 4. Rewrite and verify

1. Identify the artifact owner, intended reader, required language, and allowed sources.
2. Mark the literal and semantic content that must survive unchanged.
3. Map the fact–support–inference sequence and diagnose patterns by paragraph.
4. Rewrite the unit around its substantive point; do not patch watched words one by one.
5. Compare the revision with its sources.
   Restore every dropped qualifier, status, citation, path, command, and adverse result, and remove every new claim.
6. Run the checks required by the owning workflow and confirm that the next reader can find the decision, open item, source, file, or command they need.

A revision is ready only when traceability, state, uncertainty, causality, terminology, and ownership remain intact.
